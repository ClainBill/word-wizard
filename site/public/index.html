acrostic — cabalGames01

A daily crossword, seeded from your own words.crossword

/ˈkrɒswɜːd/ · noun · 5×5 · 9×9 · 17×17

Every day brings a Mini, a Midi and a Mega, built to American convention — 180° symmetry, no entry under three letters, every square checked, one connected grid — and anchored on the words you’re studying, with a theme that follows the day of the week. Solving a clue counts as a review: producing a word from its definition is exactly what a flashcard would ask of you.

  • ¶No initialisms, no crosswordese. The fill comes from about 70,000 real, familiarity-rated words.
  • ¶An editor with taste. Premium puzzles get an AI editorial pass that rewrites clunky clues in plain English and swaps out unfair answers.
  • ¶Typing skips filled squares and jumps to the next word when one is done.
  • ¶Stuck? Check a letter, a word or the grid — or reveal one. A reveal marks the puzzle as helped, so it scores nothing on the leaderboards.
  • ¶Want another? Each size has one fresh start a day, edited like the first. History keeps every puzzle, and progress is saved on every keystroke.
  • ¶Try it free. Everyone gets a sample Mini; the daily three are part of Premium.

Trace your words through a board.word hunt

/hʌnt/ · noun · 4×4 · one theme a day

The second daily game is a sixteen-letter board: drag through adjacent tiles to find words. Each board is built around up to two of your study words — due cards first — and one word from the day’s theme, placed so they can share tiles. If an anchor won’t fit, the board says which one it dropped rather than quietly claiming it.

  • ¶Finding a study word shows its definition — but never grades the card. Seeing the letters isn’t recall.
  • ¶Main words vs bonus words. Completion is measured against the ~40 familiar words on the board; the other 170,000 spellings the board recognises score, but are never required.
  • ¶36 themes — music, law, at sea, chemistry — one per calendar day; every theme word lights up when found.
  • ¶Fixed answer sets. Each board is solved completely when it’s made and saved with the puzzle, so a dictionary update can never move the goalposts under a game in progress.

cabal — coterieGroups02

Pool your words. See who knows them best.groups

/ɡruːps/ · noun, plural · up to twelve

Start a group, choose what it plays — a crossword at the size you like (Mini, Midi or Mega) or a Word Hunt board — and pass round its invite code. Everyone who switches on Share my words adds the words they’re studying to a common pool, and each day the pool becomes one puzzle the whole group solves, the same for everyone.

  • ¶Only headwords are shared — never your notes, pinned meanings or lookups. Switch sharing off and your words leave the pool at once.
  • ¶One puzzle a day, polished once. Whoever opens it first builds it and the AI editor goes over the clues; everyone else gets that same puzzle.
  • ¶Anyone signed in can join with a code and share words; starting a group and playing its puzzle are part of Premium. Up to five groups each.
  • ¶A leaderboard for the group — today’s scores, plus everyone’s points this week and all time. Points reward right answers, finishing, finishing unaided, and pace.
  • ¶And a public one, if you want it. Pick a public name and your puzzle points go on the weekly and all-time board for everyone.

coterie — lexiconDictionary03

740,000 entries, and not one needs a signal.dictionary

/ˈdɪkʃənəri/ · noun · the whole book, in your pocket

The English Wiktionary — every sense, origin, example and inflection — ships inside the app, so a lookup works in airplane mode, on a train, in a basement. Every entry reads in the same fixed order, and every word inside it is its own lookup: a definition, an etymology, an example sentence.

  • ¶Keep the word, or just one meaning of it. Add to Lexery puts it in your study pool; hold a sense to make that the one the flashcard asks about.
  • ¶Hear it in whichever of your phone’s offline English voices you like best.
  • ¶Spellings fold together — colour and color, shew and show are one card, not two.
  • ¶A word of the day opens the Search tab, grown into a crossword-style board you can pan around — tiles tinted by what’s already in your Lexery.

Copy anything. Open the tab. It’s a page.reader

/ˈriːdə/ · noun · a page that answers back

The Reader pulls in whatever is on your clipboard the moment you open it — headings and bold survive — and lays it out like a sheet of paper. Then it does the thing paper can’t:

  • ¶Words in your lists glow in that list’s highlighter colour.
  • ¶Words in your Lexery are underlined; uncommon words get a dotted line — so you see where your gaps are before you tap.
  • ¶Tap for a peek panel with senses and IPA. Long-press for the full entry. Any inflected form finds its lemma.
  • ¶History keeps every text you’ve read; you can also write one by hand.

lucid — lustrousLexery & lists05

Keep the ones worth keeping.lexery

/ˈlɛksəri/ · noun · your own growing store of words

Your Lexery is every word you’ve decided to learn — added from search, the Reader or a puzzle — and it’s what the flashcards and the daily games are built from. Lists sit alongside for organising: a list can hold words and other lists, nested as deep as you like, and ▶ Study on a folder adds every word inside it, at any depth, to your Lexery. Pause, reset or remove a whole subtree the same way.

  • ¶A note per word, in Markdown, shown on the entry and on the back of its flashcard.
  • ¶A colour per list, which is what the Reader uses to light your words up.
  • ¶Due counts on every list, so the Lists tab doubles as a to-do list.

lustrous — mnemonicStudy06

Review on the day you were about to forget.study

/ˈstʌdi/ · verb · SM-2, unmodified

Word Wizard schedules with SuperMemo-2, the same family Anki grew out of. Each card carries its own ease factor; every Good multiplies the interval by it, Hard nudges by 1.2×, and Again sends it back to tomorrow. Try it — the demo on the right runs the app’s actual scheduler.

  • ¶Both directions, separately. Recognising abstruse and producing it from its definition are different memories; each gets its own card, ease and history, and siblings are spaced apart in the queue.
  • ¶You choose which meaning counts. Hold a sense on the entry to pin it to the card. Pin nothing and each session deals one or two of the word’s meanings at random — so a word with eight senses isn’t forever tested on whichever one the dictionary listed first.
  • ¶Swipe to grade — right for Good, left for Again. Undo the last grade, or mark a word you know cold as mastered.
  • ¶A recap when you stop — the grade tally, what’s still due today, and every word with the day it comes back. Misses first, tap to reread.
  • ¶Two reminders a day, each naming a real word that’s about to come due.

Eight games, each aimed at the half you missed.recall · premium

/rɪˈkɔːl/ · noun · look, cover, remember

Swipe a card up for didn’t know it and, instead of just showing the answer, a recall game runs first. It’s keyed to the side you were asked for: a word card asks you to rebuild the meaning; a meaning card asks you to produce the word. After the reveal, a second exercise works the other half, so every review touches both. None of it changes your grade — a game that fed into grading would reward guessing at tiles instead of knowing the word.

  • ¶For the word — Crack the word (tiles that give up one letter at a time, never the last for free), Fill the gap, Spell it out, Sound it out, and Guess from AI-written clues.
  • ¶For the meaning — Which meaning is it?, Rebuild the meaning (the definition shattered into chips), and Where does it belong? — three real sentences from the dictionary, only one of which wants this word.
  • ¶The wrong answers are on-topic. Distractors are drawn from half a million dictionary example sentences and ranked by shared content words, so you can’t tell them apart by subject alone — it’s a test of usage, not of style.
  • ¶Sound it out is deliberately meaning-free — syllables as beats, first letter, a rhyme read aloud. Tip-of-the-tongue research says a sound cue unsticks a recall that another meaning cue won’t.
  • ¶Every game is skippable, and a dice button deals a different one for the same card.

mnemonic — obdurateProgress07

Every number in its own units. No score.progress

/ˈprəʊɡrɛs/ · noun · what actually happened

There is no composite “vocabulary score”, no XP, no level. The one headline is words mastered — cards past 90 days with their ease intact — and everything else is reported in the unit it was measured in: reviews, minutes, days, solves.

  • ¶Retention — the share of reviews on cards three or more weeks out that you didn’t forget. The honest number, not the flattering one.
  • ¶Best times are unaided solves only, per game and per size — with words solved and time played for the ones you didn’t finish.
  • ¶Looked up more than once — words you searched for and opened again are listed with a one-tap Study. Repeat lookups are the app telling you which words to keep.
  • ¶Twelve weeks of activity — reviews, lookups and games per day — and a plain-text share card, if you want to show someone.

Free to use. Premium to play.premium

/ˈpriːmiəm/ · noun · the daily games, and no ads

The dictionary, the Reader and the flashcards are free, for good. Premium turns the words you save into daily games.

Free

Everything you need to look words up and learn them

  • The 740,000-entry dictionary, offline, with pronunciation and word origins
  • The Reader, for anything you copy
  • Your Lexery, lists and notes
  • Spaced-repetition flashcards in both directions, with reminders
  • Word of the day and the Progress screen
  • A sample Mini crossword, and joining friends’ groups
  • Ads: a banner in word entries, and now and then one at the end of a review session

Premium

Everything in Free, plus

  • Three personalized crosswords every day, with the AI editor
  • A daily Word Hunt built from your words
  • Recall games in every study session
  • Starting group puzzles and playing them with friends
  • No ads

Premium comes as a monthly or yearly subscription, or as a one-time Founding Member purchase limited to the first 500 people. Prices are shown in the App Store or Google Play before you buy. An account is optional — sign in with Apple, Google or email when you want Premium on another phone, groups or the leaderboard; one account carries Premium across iPhone and Android.
